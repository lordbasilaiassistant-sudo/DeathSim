# Consciousness at death — the central unknown

This is the question everyone actually means by "what happens when you die": **does subjective experience end, and if so, exactly when?** It is the hardest question in the repository, and intellectual honesty requires admitting how little is *measured* versus *inferred*.

## What we can measure `[ESTABLISHED]`

- The **behavioral and physiological correlates** of consciousness: responsiveness, brainstem reflexes, EEG patterns, cerebral blood flow. These reliably disappear in death (see [`../facts/brain-at-death.md`](../facts/brain-at-death.md)).
- When the brain is destroyed or its function permanently ceases, the **person** — their memory, personality, behavior, the entire functional self — is gone. This is not in dispute.

## What we cannot measure `[OPEN]`

We have no instrument that detects **subjective experience itself** — the "what it is like" from the inside. We infer consciousness in others entirely from *correlates* (they report it; they behave as if conscious; their brains light up like ours). We have never measured the experience directly, in anyone, alive or dying.

This is the **hard problem of consciousness** (Chalmers, 1995): even a complete physical account of the brain does not, on its face, explain *why* there is subjective experience at all. [Chalmers]

The consequence for death is sharp and uncomfortable:

> We can establish, with certainty, that the **functional person ends**. We cannot *directly* establish the moment **experience ends**, because we cannot directly detect experience even in the living. We infer its cessation. The inference is reasonable and parsimonious — but it is an inference, not a reading off a dial.

**Why this is `[OPEN]` and not just answered by the physicalist default:** The default answer — "experience is what brain activity does, so it ends when brain activity ends" — is the *most parsimonious* and is the working assumption of neuroscience. But it is a position on the hard problem, not a measurement that closes it. Labeling it `[ESTABLISHED]` would be exactly the smuggle this repo refuses (see [`../TAXONOMY.md`](../TAXONOMY.md), rule 4). The honest status: **the cessation of the person is `[ESTABLISHED]`; the precise fate and timing of experience is `[OPEN]`.**

## What would change this

- A validated, theory-grounded **objective measure of consciousness** (the field has candidates — e.g., the perturbational complexity index / "zap and zip," and Integrated Information Theory's Φ — but none is a settled detector of experience, and IIT itself is contested). If such a measure existed and were validated, "when does experience end" could become an empirical, answerable question.
- Until then, claims that go *beyond* "the person ends" — claims that experience definitely persists, or definitely ceases at a precise instant — are reaching past the data in opposite directions.

## The disciplined position

- ❌ "Science has proven consciousness simply switches off at death." — Overclaims; conflates the functional person (proven to end) with subjective experience (not directly measurable).
- ❌ "Science can't rule out an afterlife, so consciousness probably survives." — Misuses an epistemic gap as positive evidence.
- ✅ "The functional self demonstrably ends with brain function. Whether and exactly when subjective experience ends is not directly measurable with current tools; the parsimonious scientific default is that it ends with brain function, but that is an inference grounded in an unsolved problem, not a closed measurement."
