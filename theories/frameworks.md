# Theories — frameworks for "what happens to experience"

**Everything on this page is `[SPECULATIVE]` by definition.** These are *frameworks* for interpreting the unknowns, not findings. They are listed so the repository is complete and so readers can see where each one actually sits relative to evidence — not to endorse any of them. They range from the scientific default (well-grounded but not a closed measurement) to the empirically unsupported fringe.

Ranked roughly by evidential grounding, strongest first.

## 1. Physicalism / the standard neuroscientific view `[SPECULATIVE]` (default, best-grounded)

Consciousness is what certain brain processes *do*. When those processes permanently cease, experience ceases — there is no experiencer left to have it. Death is, for the subject, not a state one is in but the end of states.

- **Grounding:** Every known manipulation of the mind (anesthesia, injury, drugs, stimulation) maps onto brain changes. There is zero reproducible evidence of mind without a functioning brain. This is the most parsimonious view and the working assumption of neuroscience.
- **Why still `[SPECULATIVE]` here, not `[ESTABLISHED]`:** As a claim about *subjective experience* (not the functional person, which demonstrably ends), it rests on the unresolved hard problem (see [`../unknowns/consciousness-at-death.md`](../unknowns/consciousness-at-death.md)). It is the best-supported framework, but "experience is identical to / fully exhausted by brain activity" is a philosophical position, not a measured fact.

## 2. Integrated Information Theory (IIT) and similar `[SPECULATIVE]`

Consciousness corresponds to integrated information (Φ) in a system. On this view, as the brain's integration collapses in death, consciousness fades in proportion. IIT at least aims to make "how much consciousness" measurable.

- **Status:** A serious, actively researched scientific theory — and a **contested** one (a 2023 open letter from many researchers controversially called it "pseudoscience," which itself drew rebuttals). Not validated as a consciousness detector. Belongs here, not in facts.

## 3. Quantum-consciousness / Orchestrated Objective Reduction (Orch-OR) `[SPECULATIVE]` (fringe)

Penrose & Hameroff's proposal that consciousness arises from quantum computations in neuronal microtubules, with speculative implications for whether quantum information could persist or dissipate at death.

- **Status:** Highly controversial and not accepted by mainstream neuroscience or physics. The "survival" implications sometimes attached to it are not part of any established result. Fringe.

## 4. Biocentrism and consciousness-fundamental views `[SPECULATIVE]` (philosophical, not science)

Lanza's biocentrism and various idealist/panpsychist positions hold that consciousness is fundamental rather than produced by the brain, implying death is not the end of experience in the conventional sense.

- **Status:** Philosophical/metaphysical, not empirically testable as commonly stated. Makes no confirmed predictions. Cultural and philosophical interest only.

## 5. Religious, spiritual, and cultural frameworks `[SPECULATIVE]` (out of empirical scope)

The world's traditions offer rich, varied accounts — continuation of a soul, rebirth, resurrection, dissolution, ancestral presence, and many more. These are matters of faith, meaning, and culture.

- **Status:** Outside the empirical remit of this repository. Cataloged here as *theories people hold*, never adjudicated as true or false on scientific grounds. They answer a different kind of question than the one this repo measures.

## The seam, restated

The only honest scientific statement at the bottom of all of this:

> The functional person **demonstrably ends** with brain function (`[ESTABLISHED]`). The fate of subjective *experience* is not directly measurable with current tools (`[OPEN]`). The parsimonious default is that it ends too (#1 above) — well-grounded, but a framework, not a closed measurement. Everything beyond that is `[SPECULATIVE]`, and this page labels it so on every line.
