# Clinical death vs. biological death vs. brain death

"Death" is not one event. It is a cascade, and the law, medicine, and biology each draw the line in a different place. Conflating them is the first and most common error.

## Clinical death `[ESTABLISHED]`

Cessation of heartbeat and breathing (cardiopulmonary arrest). This is **reversible** within a window — that is the entire premise of CPR and defibrillation. Blood circulation and respiration stop; if restarted soon enough (typically minutes), the organism can recover with no permanent loss. [Tintinalli]

Clinical death is therefore a *state*, not a finality. A person in clinical death has not necessarily died in any irreversible sense.

## The oxygen clock `[SUPPORTED]`

Once circulation stops, the brain — about 2% of body mass but ~20% of resting oxygen consumption — loses its oxygen and glucose supply almost immediately. Consciousness is typically lost within ~10 seconds. Without restored perfusion, neuronal injury accumulates; the classic clinical teaching is that irreversible brain damage begins within roughly **4–6 minutes** of normothermic arrest. [Tintinalli]

This window is *not* a hard constant. It stretches dramatically with **hypothermia** (cold slows metabolism — documented survivals after cold-water drowning far exceed the normothermic window) and shrinks with fever, and it is being actively revised by post-mortem cellular research (see [`brain-at-death.md`](./brain-at-death.md)).

## Brain death `[ESTABLISHED]`

Irreversible cessation of **all** functions of the entire brain, **including the brainstem**. This is the medico-legal definition of death in most jurisdictions (in the US, codified by the Uniform Determination of Death Act, 1981). A brain-dead patient may have a beating heart on mechanical ventilation, yet is legally and biologically dead, because the integrating organ is permanently gone. [UDDA]

Brain death is determined by strict clinical criteria: coma with known cause, absent brainstem reflexes, and apnea, often confirmed with ancillary tests (EEG, cerebral blood-flow studies). It is distinct from — and must not be confused with — coma, persistent vegetative state, or "locked-in" syndrome, all of which involve *some* preserved brain function.

## Biological / cellular death `[ESTABLISHED]`

The progressive death of cells and tissues from oxygen deprivation (ischemia) and the breakdown that follows. Different tissues die at very different rates: neurons are among the most vulnerable, while skin, bone, and connective-tissue cells can remain viable for hours — which is why organ and tissue donation is possible *after* legal death.

Biological death is the slow, distributed finality underneath the sharp legal line. See [`body-after-death.md`](./body-after-death.md) for its visible timeline.

## Why the distinction is load-bearing

- A person can be **clinically dead and fully recoverable** (mid-CPR).
- A person can be **legally/brain dead with a beating heart** (on a ventilator).
- A body can be **legally dead while billions of its cells are still alive** (the basis of transplantation).

Any statement of the form "when you die, X happens" is ambiguous until it specifies *which* death. Most popular claims about death — especially about experience and consciousness — quietly slide between these definitions. This file exists so the rest of the repository doesn't.
