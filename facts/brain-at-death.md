# The brain at death — what the electrodes actually show

This is the frontier where settled biology and genuine unknown meet. The *electrical* events of a dying brain have been measured directly. What those events *mean for experience* has not been resolved — keep that seam visible (it lives in [`../unknowns/consciousness-at-death.md`](../unknowns/consciousness-at-death.md)).

## Loss of consciousness and the flat line `[ESTABLISHED]`

When cerebral blood flow stops, cortical electrical activity degrades within seconds. The EEG progresses toward **isoelectric** ("flat") — the absence of detectable cortical electrical activity — typically within tens of seconds of circulatory arrest. Isoelectric EEG is one of the ancillary findings used in determining brain death. That the cortex goes electrically silent without oxygen is not disputed. [Tintinalli; UDDA]

## The end-of-life gamma surge `[CONTESTED]`

A striking and genuinely surprising finding: in the seconds *after* cardiac arrest, before the flat line, the brain can show a transient **surge of synchronized gamma-band activity** — the frequency band associated, in waking brains, with conscious processing and information integration.

- **Borjigin et al., 2013 (PNAS)** — in rats, a transient surge of synchronous, highly coherent gamma oscillations within ~30 seconds after experimental cardiac arrest, *preceding* isoelectric EEG. The coherence briefly exceeded waking levels. [Borjigin2013]
- **Borjigin group, 2023 (PNAS)** — reported gamma activity and increased connectivity in the dying human brain in a small number of comatose patients after withdrawal of life support. Very small N. [Borjigin2023]
- **Vicente et al., 2022 (Front. Aging Neurosci.)** — a single 87-year-old patient who arrested during EEG monitoring. **Crucial nuance:** *absolute* gamma power did **not** rise; only the **relative** proportion of gamma increased while all bands declined. Heavily confounded — traumatic brain injury, subdural hematoma, status epilepticus, anesthetic and anticonvulsant drugs, asphyxia. Not clean evidence of a surge. [Vicente2022]

**Why `[CONTESTED]`, not `[SUPPORTED]`:** the animal finding is reproducible and real, but the human evidence is a handful of confounded case reports, and the leap from "gamma oscillations" to "conscious near-death experience" is an *interpretation*, not a measurement. The electrical event is real; its experiential meaning is unproven. This is exactly the kind of claim the [taxonomy](../TAXONOMY.md) exists to keep from inflating.

## Terminal spreading depolarization `[SUPPORTED]`

As neurons exhaust their energy supply, they undergo **spreading depolarization** — a slow wave of mass depolarization (sometimes called the "brain tsunami" or, when terminal, the "wave of death") that propagates across the cortex as the ion gradients keeping cells alive collapse. Critically, this wave marks a **commitment point but is itself reversible** if circulation is restored quickly enough — it is the toxic process, not the moment of irreversible death per se. Studied in animal models and observed in dying human cortex. [Dreier; Zandt2011]

## Cellular function can outlast "death" `[SUPPORTED]`

The assumption that brain cells die within minutes and irreversibly is now known to be too simple:

- **Vrselja et al., 2019 (Nature) — "BrainEx."** Yale researchers restored circulation and a range of **molecular and cellular functions** in isolated pig brains beginning **four hours** after death, using a perfusion system. **What it did NOT show, emphatically:** any coordinated, brain-wide electrical activity indicating awareness — the experiment was explicitly designed to detect and prevent that, and none appeared. It demonstrates that *cellular* death is slower and more interruptible than taught; it says **nothing** about restored consciousness. [Vrselja2019]

This finding matters because it dissolves the clean line between "alive" and "dead" at the cellular level, and it cautions against reading too much into any single timeline.

## The honest summary

| Claim | Tag |
|-------|-----|
| Cortex goes electrically silent (isoelectric) without oxygen | `[ESTABLISHED]` |
| A post-arrest gamma surge occurs (clear in rats) | `[SUPPORTED]` (animal) / `[CONTESTED]` (human) |
| That surge *is* a conscious near-death experience | `[SPECULATIVE]` — see [theories](../theories/frameworks.md) |
| Terminal spreading depolarization marks a reversible commitment point | `[SUPPORTED]` |
| Brain *cellular* function can be partially restored hours later | `[SUPPORTED]` |
| Any of the above restored *consciousness* | No — explicitly not shown |
